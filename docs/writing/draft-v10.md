Conventions, as draft v9. A phone event shows a caption: the kicker
"<Month Year> · <category>", the event's title, and its category's
line. A letter has none: its letterhead and date line are its heading;
this round's three letters come from houses, not papers. Replies are the
player's words; outcomes are the other side's reply (a phone) or the
result (a letter, in italic after the SENT stamp). Effects are written as
on the card faces; "−1 action" is an action spent this month; "Everyone's
Running It" and "Cash Grab" are existing scandal cards, added to the
discard pile. The effects here are the author's starting numbers; the
round tunes them with the sim, and its report lists every number changed.

THE SECOND VARIANTS — the groups shown once a run (`.2` keys)

The opening (story.opening.2), four paragraphs, a blank line between each:
- Spring.
- You have three songs you're proud of, a borrowed guitar, and a room above a launderette that's always warm.
- Everyone here is trying to get noticed. It's easier than you'd think, and it costs more than anyone tells you.
- You have one year.

Season openers
- spring: Small rooms, cheap tickets, nobody watching closely. Mistakes cost little now. They won't for long.
- summer: Long days, short nights, and everyone is out. Whatever you do this summer, someone will have their phone up.
- autumn: The nights draw in and the papers dig in. They have a file on you now, and it's growing.
- winter: The year is nearly written. The industry is reading it back, deciding what kind of story you were.

The doors' flavour
- audition: Three judges, one song, no backing track. Nobody in that room has heard of you, which is the point.
- showcase: Ten acts, one night, every booker in the city. They'll remember two of you.
- festival: A tent, a field, four thousand people waiting for the next band. Make them forget who they came for.
- session_work: A famous producer needs a voice for one line, by Monday. The credit will say "additional vocals".
- label_deal: Glass walls, good coffee, a contract on the table. They want numbers, and the money to look serious.
- residency: Every Wednesday for a season, at a venue that books on taste and reads the papers.
- arena_tour: Twenty arenas, thirty minutes a night, opening for someone enormous. The label has to sign off first.
- award_show: The committee wants talent, a name, and a promise you won't be the story on the night.

The minor endings
- star: The label threw a party when the single went top ten, and you left early to write the next one. That was the surprise: success made you hungrier. The shows sell out, the reviews are kind, and your face is on the side of a bus. Some nights you still check whether it's real. So far, it keeps being real.
- leading_role: You said yes to a favour and came out with a career. Now there are scripts on the kitchen table and a car that comes for you at five. The papers can't decide whether you're an actor who sings or a singer who acts. You've stopped helping them decide. On set, nobody asks.
- household_name: Your face sells shampoo in three countries. People quote your interviews at each other. You can't remember the last room where nobody knew you, and you've stopped looking for one. It's a strange job, being known for being known, and you're very good at it. The trick is never letting anyone see how much work it takes.
- the_independent: No label signed you, so you signed yourself. You booked the rooms, printed the shirts and answered your own emails at two in the morning. Now the rooms are bigger, and the emails come from people who once ignored you. You don't owe anyone a second album. Nobody can take back what nobody gave you.
- redemption_arc: There was a month when every headline was a scandal, and most of them were yours. You didn't hide. You apologised where it was owed, fixed what you could, and kept turning up. Slowly the papers changed their tone, then their story. They'll call it a comeback. You'll call it the year you learned to stay standing.
- tabloid_royalty: The Flash has a desk just for you now, and you keep it busy. A feud on Monday, a new look by Wednesday, a cryptic post at midnight for the weekend. You learned to feed the papers before they could feed on you. It's a performance like any other, and the curtain never comes down. You knew that when you took the part.
- meltdown: You became the story everyone was waiting for, then the one nobody could stop telling. The music is in there somewhere, under the headlines. Your phone lights up all night, mostly with people who want a quote. You're famous, and you can't walk to the shop. Some days that trade looks fair. Some days you'd give it all back for one quiet week.
- character_actor: You're the face audiences can't quite place and directors can't stop casting. Small parts all year, each better than it had any right to be. Nobody stops you for selfies. Casting directors stop you in corridors. It's a quieter life than the posters promise, and it has a way of lasting longer.
- the_one_to_watch: You've been to every launch and every party, and the cameras have started finding you without being told where to look. Not famous yet. Close enough to feel the warmth of it. It's the strangest kind of fame: almost. Next year decides whether almost becomes something, or becomes a story about almost.
- craftsman: Your name doesn't sell tickets yet. It sells songs for other people, which turns out to be a living. You're the one they ring at midnight when the chorus isn't working, and you always pick up. The people who know, know. Some careers are loud. Yours is going to be long.
- nobody: You played the rooms and wrote the songs, and the year went by without quite noticing. The papers wrote about you now and then, and moved on. It stings. But nobody has made up their mind about you yet, and that cuts both ways. You're still here, and still free to become anyone.
- flash_in_the_pan: There was a week when you couldn't open an app without seeing yourself. Then the stories turned, and the photos got less kind. You still have the clippings, good and bad. You also have next year, which is more than the clippings can say.
- running_on_empty: Somewhere between the late nights and the early calls, the joy went quiet. You kept saying yes because stopping felt like losing. Now your voice is tired, the diary is full, and you can't remember the last song you wrote for fun. Nobody is going to give you permission to stop. You'll have to give it to yourself.
- starting_over: You chased the shortcuts, and the shortcuts went the long way round. The headlines you wanted didn't stick, and the ones you didn't want did. But the year taught you more than a good one would have. You know who calls back, who doesn't, and what you'd never do again. That's a start, and it's yours.
